    📖 Words to know

    Cash
    The money your club has. You start with $250,000. If cash is below $0 for two quarters in a row, the game ends.
    Revenue streams
    The different ways a club earns money: sponsors, prize money, and fans (tickets, merch and streams).
    Fixed costs
    Costs that are the same every quarter, like salaries. A new hire adds to your fixed costs for good.
    Variable costs
    Costs that change with your choices (marketing, training, events), plus travel, penalties and prize shares.
    Break-even
    Earning at least as much as you spend in a quarter. Money in must be as big as fixed costs plus variable costs.
    Base salary
    A player’s guaranteed pay. It is a fixed cost: you pay it whether the team wins or loses.
    Prize share
    A percent of prize money paid to players. It is a variable cost: it only costs you when you win.
    Sponsorship deal
    A flat deal pays the same every quarter. A performance deal pays more for each win. A fan-based deal pays more as your fanbase grows.
    Activations
    What sponsors expect in return: posts, event appearances and logos on gear. Good activations keep sponsors happy.
    TSR (team skill)
    How good your players are, from 0 to 100.
    Morale
    How happy your players are, from 0 to 100. Low morale lowers your chance to win. At 0 the players quit.
    Fans
    Your fanbase. You need fans to move up a tier.
    Tier
    Your club’s rank: Tier 3 Rookie, Tier 2 Challenger, Tier 1 Elite. Higher tiers pay bigger prizes.
    Tournament
    One each quarter. A win pays prize money and adds 1,500 fans. A loss costs $10,000 and 5% of your fans.
    Sponsorship manager
    A hire you must make by Quarter 3. It costs $12,000, plus $6,000 every quarter. Sponsors only sign deals with clubs that have one, and they pay $3,500 more every quarter.
